# Helmet design references

These are simplified chess silhouettes, not archaeological reconstructions. Civilization colors are game palettes rather than claims about historical materials. Facial features remain absent; dark apertures and grilles represent helmet construction.

- **Sparta:** an Archaic/Classical Corinthian-type hoplite helmet, with integrated cheek coverage and a nasal guard. This is a selected period reference, not a claim that all Spartans always wore this type. Met, bronze helmet, sixth–fifth century BCE: https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/250921
- **Gladiator:** a murmillo-inspired large visored helmet, represented with a broad brim, raised crest and perforated face protection. Gladiator equipment varied by class; this does not represent every gladiator. British Museum, murmillo figurine: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1899-1010-1 and helmet: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/G_1946-0514-1
- **Praetorian:** an early-imperial Roman military helmet-inspired silhouette with a domed bowl, brow reinforcement, cheek guards and neck protection. This is not a uniquely attested Praetorian uniform reconstruction. British Museum Roman helmet, domed bowl and broad neck guard, missing original cheek pieces: https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/H_1813-0313-1

Small details and proportions are adapted for legibility on a chessboard. Existing weapons and shields are outside this helmet-only revision.
